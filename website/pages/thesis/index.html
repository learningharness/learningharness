The missing step
is your own reasoning.

Guidance helps you work beyond what you can do alone. Working through that zone can turn supported ability into independent understanding.

AI can deliver the result without that step. Following its explanation may feel like understanding. Can you still reason through it when one condition changes?

Make that reach
your own.

LearningHarness brings guided learning back alongside work that AI can finish without you.

  1. Find a next step. Relevant work and learning history help LH notice an opportunity before you think to ask.
  2. Reason through it. Guided teaching, a changed case and feedback make room for your own thinking.
  3. Return to it. Saved attempts help shape later practice.

Your coding agent keeps working. LH reads the work; it doesn’t edit it or send your practice answers back. You choose when to learn.

The ideas behind the thesis

Lev Vygotsky’s zone of proximal development concerns what a learner can do with guidance beyond their independent ability. We use it as a lens for product design, not a measurement of adult developer expertise.

  • Gordon Wells on Vygotsky’s zone of proximal development
  • Soderstrom and Bjork: learning versus performance
  • Karpicke and Blunt: retrieval practice and meaningful learning
  • Anthropic: AI assistance and coding-skill formation
  • Addy Osmani on critical thinking with agents

These sources inform our understanding of the problem. They are not evaluations or endorsements of LearningHarness.
